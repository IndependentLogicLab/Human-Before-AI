# Human Before AI

### An IndependentLogicLab Research Project

**Research status:** Planning and preliminary investigation  
**Project established:** November 2026  
**Research type:** Independent computational and statistical research  
**Target system:** GPTZero 5.0  
**Funding:** No external funding

---

## Abstract

Human Before AI is an independent research project investigating potential systematic false positives in AI-generated text detection.

Using verifiably human-authored historical publications, this project aims to determine whether specific linguistic characteristics, publication periods, writing styles, and subject areas influence AI detection accuracy.

The research emphasizes transparency, reproducibility, authentic source material, and evidence-based conclusions.

## 1. Research Objectives

- Identify reproducible false positives in AI-generated text detection.
- Investigate potential relationships between writing characteristics and detection scores.
- Compare detection accuracy across historical periods and subject areas.
- Determine whether false positives represent isolated incidents or systematic patterns.
- Publish documented findings that contribute to improving AI detection technology.

## 2. Research Hypotheses

### Null Hypothesis (H0)

AI detection false-positive rates do not differ significantly across the historical writing categories being investigated.

### Alternative Hypothesis (H1)

Certain characteristics of authentic human writing may be associated with significantly higher false-positive rates.

Potential contributing characteristics include:

- Sentence structure consistency.
- Predictable vocabulary.
- Formal academic language.
- Repetitive instructional structures.
- Technical terminology.
- Historical grammatical conventions.
- Limited linguistic variation.

These hypotheses remain untested.

Potential source repositories include:

- Project Gutenberg

- Google Books
- HathiTrust Digital Library

Every included sample must have independently verifiable publication information and evidence of human authorship.

## 5. Sample Requirements

Each primary research sample should:

- Contain approximately 750–1,500 consecutive words.
- Preserve the original published wording.
- Have a verifiable publication date.
- Include documented authorship where available.
- Include a publicly accessible source reference.
- Exclude AI-generated, machine-translated, or artificially manipulated passages.
- Exclude duplicate and overlapping samples where practical.
- Be checked against original publication scans when transcription accuracy is uncertain.

## 6. Research Methodology

### Phase 1: Source Collection

Identify eligible historical publications and record their bibliographic information.

### Phase 2: Dataset Preparation

Extract consecutive passages using documented, consistent sampling procedures.

Assign each sample a unique identifier and preserve its source reference.

### Phase 3: Automated Testing

Use Python and authorized GPTZero API access to submit eligible samples and record detection results.

### Phase 4: Statistical Analysis

Analyze:

- Overall false-positive rates.
- Category-specific false-positive rates.
- Detection score distributions.
- Publication date relationships.
- Passage length relationships.
- Vocabulary diversity.
- Sentence length variation.
- Linguistic predictability.
- Potential systematic detection weaknesses.
